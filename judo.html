2022 Founding

There was no club, so I started one.

The University of Michigan didn't have a judo club. Starting one turns out to be far less about judo than you'd think — it's paperwork, insurance, university approvals, a room you're allowed to use at hours people will actually come, and ten founding members you have to find before the university will recognise you exist.

We got the ten. Four years on there are around fifty.

2022 Logistics

The mats came from China. I got them off the boat.

A judo club is a floor before it is anything else. You cannot throw people onto a gym floor, and a domestic tatami order was far beyond what a brand new student group could fund.

So I went around it. I sourced the mats from a manufacturer in China and negotiated the order directly — 64 square metres of tatami, about 690 square feet, for roughly $5,000. Then came the part nobody warns you about: arranging international freight, getting the import documentation right, clearing customs, and physically collecting a pallet of mats from a port. Then laying the floor.

It was the first project I ever ran end to end, and I've since done fellowship work for two branches of the armed forces without anything feeling quite as difficult.

2022 — present Head coach · volunteer

Seven team championships.

I've coached the club since the day it opened, unpaid, as a volunteer. In that time the team has won three state championships, two regional championships and two national collegiate championships.

The number I'm prouder of is quieter: our first competition entry was three people. We now send twenty-four. Most of them had never done judo before they walked into our room, and the job that actually matters is the route between those two states — taking someone who can't yet fall safely and getting them onto a competition mat without either injuring them or frightening them off.

  • 3× State champions Team · as head coach
  • 2× Regional champions Team · as head coach
  • 2× National collegiate champions Team · as head coach
2022 — present Competitor · ikyu

Three state titles, back to back.

I compete as well as coach. Three consecutive state titles at the Meijer State Games of Michigan under the United States Judo Federation, across 2022, 2023 and 2024, and third place at the NCJA College and High School Championships in 2024.

Six years on the mat, currently ikyu. Running the club has cost me some of my own training time — you cannot coach a session and take it at the same time — and my grading has moved slower than it would have otherwise. That's a trade I'd make again.

  • 3× State champion Meijer State Games of Michigan · USJF
  • 3rd National collegiate NCJA College & High School Championships, 2024
